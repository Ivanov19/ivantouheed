1. Machine-Learning & DFT-Guided Electrode Design for Oxide-based Supercapacitors: From Data Mining to Experimental Validation [Ongoing work]

The compositional space of supercapacitor electrode materials is far too large for trial-and-error synthesis to explore meaningfully. None of the conventional routes leading to a candidate validation comes cheap; synthesis consumes physical resources and laboratory time, while first-principles validation consumes computational resources and time of its own. This work, instead, proposes a pipeline where published literature data-space is utilised as a screening asset to prepare machine-learning based predictive models that perform as inexpensive statistical filters before committing to any resource-intensive step. The intention behind the statistical screening stages is simply to confidently eliminate the subsets of the huge available material-space, so that DFT validation and synthesis are spent only on the most promising candidates.

The first stage is computational. A descriptor dataset is prepared from the existing experimental literatures, with a feature space spanning chemistry, structure, synthesis route and testing conditions. This dataset trains a machine-learning model that ranks candidate compositions by supercapacitive performance, while SHAP and partial-dependence analyses expose the physical drivers underlying those rankings rather than leaving the model opaque. A second model screens the shortlist for stability, so that first-principles validation is directed only at candidates likely to survive it.

The second stage is experimental. Selected candidates surviving computational validation are synthesized toward the nanoscale regime in which near-surface Faradaic storage dominates, fabricated into electrodes, and assessed through structural and electrochemical characterizations. The resulting measurements are returned to the dataset, closing the loop and refining subsequent rounds of screening. The whole pipeline is, as we call, "A Computation-guided Closed Loop Pipeline".

The long-term aim of this project is generality. The architecture is not specifically restricted to supercapacitors; it has been instantiated for ABO₃ perovskite materials because that is where our screening and characterization capacity currently lies, but the same structure extends to other properties and applications. Given synthesis and characterization can themselves be automated, the loop closes without even any human intervention, pointing toward a self-driving laboratory!

I have another work (a personal project; aimed at a much smaller scale) that simulates a similar self-driving design automation architecture for a MEMS Pressure Microsensor Device, if you feel interested you can check here.

2. Data-driven Optimisation of Manganese–Vanadium Oxide Battery Electrodes [Currently under planning]

The lab has spent a substantial amount of time conducting experiments to improve the performance of manganese–vanadium oxide (Mn–V–O) based batteries and has accumulated a collection of raw experimental data. Improvements seem to have since plateaued, and further gains from intuition alone appear increasingly hard-won.

We are to introduce a data-driven statistical analysis to identify which compositional and processing variables actually govern energy density, and guide the next rounds of experiments bypassing the current trial-and-error approaches.

LC-Based Capacitive MEMS Pressure Microsensor 2026

Independent project · Python simulation + ML-based inverse design

Built an end-to-end Python simulation of a passive, wirelessly-interrogated capacitive MEMS pressure sensor — modelling the full chain from membrane deflection to LC resonance and inductive readout. Trained a neural-network surrogate on 171 simulated geometries (sampled via Latin-Hypercube) and used inverse design to optimise the sensor's geometry.
